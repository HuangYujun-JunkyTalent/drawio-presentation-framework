Slides building principles:

1. Keep all the formats consistent. PDF, HTML, and other built formats should have the same display whenever possible.
2. Keep the editor and the built formats consistent. What humans see and interact with in the editor should be consistent with what is built and displayed in the final formats.
   1. Especially for the html format, which should be as powerful as possible and consistent with the editor, so that the presenter can use it to present and interact as in the editor.
3. Illustrations that do not require true data and/or computation should be editable in the editor. Only those plots requiring true data and/or computation should be generated and imported from, for instance, jupyter notebooks. Balance two points: (1) the presenter should be able to edit and interact with the illustrations in the editor without having to regenerate them, and (2) the presenter should not have to do too much work to adapt the illustrations if new data or computation changes the illustrations.

Slides content principles:

1. Only keep those types of content:
  1. things that the audience can instantly understand, without needing to read a lot of text or listen to a long explanation.
  2. things that presenter has to explain, and it's worth knowing and is not obvious to the audience.
2. Avoid content that is:
  1. too detailed, technical, or complex for the audience to understand quickly, and also not worth explaining in detail.
3. Use running examples that are relevant and easy to understand for the audience
   1. Preferably, show examples and intuitions before showing the formal definitions, results, and theorems.
   2. Use animations to illustrate the examples when possible, even interactively.
   3. If the audience is specific, use examples that are relevant to their background and interests.

A skill that can polish the slides:

1. Title rules:
  1. every slide has a clear and concise title, centered, with proper font, capitalization, and size.
  2. titles should be consistent in style across all slides, using the same font family and weight.
2. Outline and conclusion slides:
  1. include an outline slide at the beginning to provide an overview of the presentation.
  2. include a conclusion slide at the end to summarize key points and takeaways, together with future work and discussion points.
  3. Acknowledgements and references should be included in the conclusion slide or showed separately in a dedicated slide, if necessary.
  4. No simple "Thank you" slide at the end. It's a waste of time, space, and attention.
3. Alignment of lists and bullet points:
  1. ensure that all lists and bullet points in various slides are properly aligned and formatted for readability.
  2. Keep the style of lists and bullet points consistent across all slides, using the same indentation, bullet style, and spacing.
4. Alignment of diagrams and images:
  1. ensure that all diagrams and images are properly aligned with the text and other elements on the slide.
  2. maintain consistent margins and spacing around diagrams and images to create a balanced layout.
  3. try to rearrange diagrams and images to avoid clutter and improve visual flow if necessary.
  4. For those images or diagrams that are listed (like logos or icons), ensure they are aligned in a proper format in context, with proper size and spacing. Consult the style guide for specific alignment rules or organizations if applicable. For instance, there's guidelines to place MIT logos: <https://brand.mit.edu/logos-marks>
5. Alignment of related diagrams and images:
  1. for those slides that share some contents (foe instance, some slides have contents appearing one after another), ensure that those same diagrams and images are aligned in the same way across those slides.
  2. Make sure the related contents are aligned in a consistent manner. For instance, if tables of comparison are used across multiple slides, even with different data, ensure that the tables are aligned in the same way across those slides.
6. Spell, grammar, and punctuation:
  1. proofread all slides for spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
  2. use a consistent style for capitalization, abbreviations, and acronyms throughout the presentation.
  3. For slide titles, only capitalize the first letter of the fThe irst word, unless some formatting style requires otherwise.
